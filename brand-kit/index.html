01

Essence

"We believe the most memorable wines come from small, family-run vineyards, where heritage is honored and every bottle tells a story."
Source: Sunnyside Vines homepage
Named, not generic
Every wine belongs to a grower with a name and a hillside. The brand never speaks of "Spain" in the abstract.
Handmade, not manufactured
Marks look stamped, photos look shot on location, edges look slightly rough. Nothing reads as templated.
Warm, not loud
Terracotta and paper tones, golden-hour light, one adjective instead of three. The wine does the talking.

02

The Mark

A postage-stamp icon, perforated edges around a sunrise and a wine glass, paired with custom lettering. The icon can stand alone once the brand is established; the wordmark carries the name when it can't.

Clear space
Keep space equal to the mark's own width open on every side. Nothing else sits inside that margin: no type, no edge of frame.
Minimum size
Below roughly 24px the perforated edge and the wine glass lose their definition. Drop to the wordmark alone instead of shrinking further.
Don't stretch or squeeze the lockup to fit a space.
Don't recolor the wordmark outside ink, terra, or white.
Don't drop it on a busy photo with no scrim behind it.

03

Color

Terracotta earth and paper stock: the color of Rioja soil and an old label, not a swatch chosen for contrast ratios. Terra is the one accent; everything else is a neutral built to sit near it without competing.

04

Typography

Fraunces speaks for the brand; it has the slight irregularity of hand-set type. Hanken Grotesk stays out of the way underneath it.

05

Voice & Tone

The brand describes wine the way a grower would over a kitchen table: specific, unhurried, no salesmanship.

Name the place
"Limestone slopes above the river" beats "beautiful terroir." A real hillside, a real distance, a real family name, always chosen over an abstract claim.
One adjective, not three
No stacked praise, no exclamation points. If a wine is remarkable, say "remarkable" once and move on to the fact that proves it.
People before product
Casimiro, Michael Cooper: growers are named like colleagues, not credited like suppliers.
Numbers over hype
"Since the 17th century." "Four kilometres further up the valley." A specific fact does the convincing that an adjective can't.
Organic Tempranillo from limestone slopes above the river, aged long in oak. Casimiro's family has pressed wine in Peñafiel's cellars since the 17th century.

06

Photography

Shot on location, in real light. Never staged in a studio, never stock.

Actual location photography used on the site: a cave cellar in Ribera del Duero, and vine rows at golden hour.

Do

Shoot at the edges of the day. Let the sun flare, let shadows fall long.

Do

Show the work: barrels, hands, rock, dirt. The place should look worked, not staged.

Don't

Use flat midday light or anything that reads as a studio product shot.

Don't

Over-retouch. A little grain and imperfection is the point, not a flaw.
